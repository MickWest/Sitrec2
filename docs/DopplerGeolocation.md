# Doppler geolocation (admin preview)

Open **Main → Extra Tools → Doppler Geolocation**. This experimental tool compares stationary-listener aircraft sound with timestamped flight tracks. Results are ranked candidate matches; a high score is not, by itself, a verified location.

The interface, audio analysis, search engine and archive reader load on demand. Audio and flight files remain in the browser. Search calculations run in a worker. Archive searches may fetch aircraft type classifications and public terrain tiles.

## Prepare an analysis

1. Load local audio. The spectrogram uses the FFT shared with Sitrec's audio spectrum view, with browser anti-alias resampling before analysis.
2. Load flight CSV, copy loaded Sitrec tracks, or select every split part of one daily readsb archive. Archive search defaults to the continental US; change the bounds for another region.
3. Mark a flyover window, heard closest-approach time, fundamental frequency and broad speed/slant-range bounds. Trace the visible ridge, or choose spectrogram brightness scoring. Tag each traced point with its harmonic number. A second tone family from the same aircraft, such as an engine beside a rotor, takes its own family number: it receives its own fitted fundamental but shares the flight geometry and clock. The fundamental sweep applies to the main family (0). Traced-ridge scoring fits a further family without a sweep limit; spectrogram scoring searches the same fraction of the fundamental that the family's own traced points give. Long traces that include the approaching and receding tails constrain speed and slant range far better than a few points near closest approach.
4. For an unknown recording clock, use two flyovers from the same recording. A single flyover requires a recording start in UTC, including `Z` or an explicit offset.
5. Search, inspect alternative candidates, and compare predicted curves against the observed signal. The legend entries under the plot are checkboxes that show or hide the traced points, the model preview, the candidate curves and the reference curves, so the raw spectrogram can be inspected. Check independent evidence before treating a candidate as identified.

CSV columns are `flight,time,lat,lon,altitudeM`. Time must be Unix seconds or ISO time with a zone. Altitude must be metres above the WGS84 ellipsoid. Optional columns are `callsign,registration,aircraftType,groundSpeedMS,headingDegrees,verticalSpeedMS`. Pressure altitude is not automatically interchangeable with ellipsoid height.

## Physical model and constraints

The model interpolates source positions in ECEF and solves the sound-travel equation at each received sample. Doppler frequency uses radial velocity at emission time. Reported navigation velocities are preferred when available; otherwise velocity comes from position differences. All events share one recording clock; fundamental frequencies are fitted separately for each event and each tone family.

One sound speed applies to the full sound path, for the travel delay and for the Doppler factor. The default, 343 m/s, is the speed in dry air at 20 °C. At an air temperature of T °C the speed is approximately 331.3 × √(1 + T / 273.15) m/s: for example, 337 m/s at 9 °C and 347 m/s at 26 °C. Air along the path to an aircraft is usually colder than air at the ground. Set the sound speed from the temperature at the time of the recording. The order of closely scored candidates can change by a few m/s of sound speed, mostly when the traces show only one side of the closest approach. An archive search therefore refines its best flight pairs again with the sound speed lowered and raised by the **Sound-speed check** value (default 5 m/s, over the 20 best pairs; 0 turns it off) and reports the rank of the winner at each speed, and how many pairs could not be refined there. A winner that does not stay first, or that is compared with pairs that could not be refined, is not established by the audio. Wind is not modelled; it changes the effective sound speed along the path.

Brightness scoring samples the entire allowed fundamental-frequency band finely enough to resolve narrow spectrogram ridges, including the selected harmonic. The minimum frequency sample setting can increase this density. Traced-ridge scoring fits frequency directly to the observations. With **Trace residuals: Squared**, the fit minimizes the mean squared residual. With **Robust**, it uses a pseudo-Huber loss: residuals smaller than the trace sigma count as squared, larger ones grow only linearly, so a few traced points from another sound pull the fit less. The listed RMS is always the plain root mean square residual. A long section of a trace that follows another sound still spoils the fit with either loss; remove it from the trace.

Archive discovery uses sparse speed and timing bounds, exact segmented closest approaches, and continuous terrain refinement before global ranking. The coarse grid proposes starting locations; the optimizer can move between grid points. When a refinement's best fit reaches the edge of its search box, the box is re-centred there and the refinement repeats a bounded number of times, so a proposal a few kilometres from the optimum still reaches it. With at least four valid traced observations in every event, brightness searches use the traces to propose and initialize locations, then score every surviving region against the spectrogram. No coarse ranking cutoff decides the final match. Known jets can be excluded as event candidates. The aircraft type list ships with Sitrec (`data/aircraft/icao_aircraft_types.json`) and contains information from the [Mictronics aircraft database](https://github.com/Mictronics/aircraft-database), which is made available under the [ODC Attribution License](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/by/1-0/). By default, the optional silence check uses the same aircraft type filter, without the event speed limits. Enable “Include excluded aircraft types in silence checks” to test all retained traffic instead. The archive retains these aircraft, so changing the silence scope does not require reindexing.

An archive search scores many flight pairs, and at most one of them is right. After the search, the winner is compared with the best scores of the other pairs, which show how well a wrong answer can fit this recording. Pairs that share a flight with the winner are left out. With silence windows, only pairs that pass the silence check count: for each pair, the search checks its refined regions in score order until one passes. The best 5 % of the other pairs (at least 15, at most 100) are taken as an exponential tail, and p is the chance that a wrong winner would lead the next pair by the observed gap. The result is "located" for p below 0.1, "ambiguous" below 0.5 and otherwise "no match". This screen is not calibrated and p is not the probability that the location is correct. Two flyovers often give "ambiguous" or "no match" even when the winner is right; further flyovers on the same clock separate a right answer more clearly. The method is from [dopplertrack](https://github.com/mjshiggins/dopplertrack) (MIT License).

Silence windows reject candidates with an intervening close flight. A radius of zero uses the furthest matched closest approach. This constraint depends on flight-data coverage and the assumption that those other flights would have been audible.

Archive terrain heights combine public terrain with the EGM96 geoid. Geometric flight altitude is preferred. Pressure-altitude fallback is explicit and approximate. Gaps beyond the selected maximum are not interpolated; near-sonic segments or implausible position jumps are rejected.

## Save and inspect

Saved analyses retain input measurements, spectrogram settings, selected track snippets, candidate rankings, selection and source-file information. Local audio and archive files need to be reselected after reopening. Reloading the same audio with the saved spectrogram settings preserves its results. Heat maps are regenerated by a search. CSV export contains the ranked candidate locations.

In Results, select a candidate and choose **Export labelled PNG**. The image combines each event's spectrogram with its predicted Doppler curve and traced observations. Labels include callsign, aircraft type, registration, flight ID, fitted frequency, closest slant distance, speed and UTC sound-arrival time where available. It also records the listener position, shared recording clock, alternative candidates and source files. Reload the original audio before exporting a reopened analysis. The image renderer loads only when export is requested.

The archive index is cached while its window stays open. Closing the window releases it. Stopping an active search currently terminates the worker and requires reindexing. Large audio files can also require substantial memory during browser decoding.

The default archive search proposes locations on a 2 km grid, continuously refines every surviving region, and draws detailed maps for the best five candidates. The final coordinate is not restricted to that grid. When every event has at least four traced points, the coarse stage tries the shared clock at steps of at least 4 s, then tries one more clock where a parabola through the best step and its two neighbors has its peak. Spectrogram scoring without traces uses steps of at least 2 s. A smaller coarse step costs more time and can help difficult recordings. The whole-day index can require about 1 GB before other browser memory, so start with narrower bounds when they are known.

A complete continental-US reference search with two measured flyovers ranked the matching aircraft pair first, approximately 38 m from the [reference demonstration's](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UytYrhYLOKI) published two-event estimate. It took 607 seconds, including about 94 seconds for indexing, on the development machine using the delivered JavaScript engine with Node file and PNG-decoding adapters. This is not a browser timing or an independent measurement of error to the microphone. The browser workflow was separately checked with the original audio, restored results and labelled PNG export. The video's faint single-event example remains unverified. A reported candidate remains dependent on the supplied measurements and archive coverage.
