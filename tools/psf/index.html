The .psf.json carries the PSF as an RGBE-encoded PNG — three 8-bit mantissas and a shared exponent, holding the full ~13 decades of range — plus the generator spec, so reopening it here restores every control. Sitrec imports the same file into a camera.

The range floor discards pixels below a fraction of the peak. Most of the file is the deep tail, which is incompressible noise far below anything a display can show: measured on the 512² Chandelier, flooring at 1e-7 takes the PNG from 635 kB to 80 kB and loses 0.11% of the flux, while the faintest structure worth looking at sits at 1e-6.

Fraunhofer model: the image-plane amplitude of a point source is the Fourier transform of the complex pupil, so the PSF is |FFT(mask · eiφ)|² with φ = 2π·W20·ρ²/λ carrying defocus. Wavelength does not change the transform, only the scale it lands at — which is why the spikes are spectra, and why an in-focus polychromatic PSF costs one transform rather than one per wavelength. Method after the Maskulator + FFTW analysis of the "Chandelier" image on Metabunk.
