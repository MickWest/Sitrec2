# The rate-jump statistic, measured over every recorded scenario

Finding F9 of `BOT-Tractability-Study.md` reports a lead: the dossier's
kinematic profile computes `jumpRatio` — the largest single-frame jump in
sightline angular rate as a multiple of the median rate — and on the escalation
pilot the two spliced-impulse anomalies read 0.6 and 0.3 while the mundane Go
Fast control read 0.2 and the radiosonde-burst false-positive probe also read
0.2. The statistic is computed from the sightlines alone, with no fit and no
assumed class, which is exactly the property the anomaly work needs.

This note is the measurement F9 asked for: the same statistic over all 26
recorded scenarios instead of five.

**It does not separate.** At the pre-registered threshold, 5/6 declared
anomalies alarm and 14/20 mundane scenarios alarm — Fisher exact two-sided
p = 1.0. What the statistic tracks on this set is the clip's SAMPLE RATE, not
whether the target is doing anything unusual.

This is a negative result about one summary number, not about the kinematic
profile. The time-resolved table the profile publishes is still what shows an
analyst where a discontinuity sits, and that is what it was added for. Nothing
in `lib/dossier.js` changed.

## How to reproduce

    npx jest benchmarks/botbench/jumpStatistic.bench.test.js \
        --testPathIgnorePatterns /node_modules/ --forceExit

Full per-scenario table, intervals and sweep:
`results/tractability/jump-statistic.md` (regenerated by that command; the
`results/` tree is not committed). Source:
`benchmarks/botbench/jumpStatistic.bench.test.js`.

It is a bench test for the usual reason: the statistic needs the OBSERVED
sightlines, records store summaries rather than series, so every scenario is
regenerated through `generateScenario` — and that pulls in the whole lib graph,
whose extensionless imports plain Node ESM will not resolve.

## The threshold was fixed before the outcome was seen

The obvious way to manufacture a result here is to pick the threshold after
seeing which value splits the classes. The threshold used is therefore not
chosen in this work at all. It is lifted from an assertion already committed to
`tests/botbench/kinematicProfile.test.js`:

    expect(stepped.jumpRatio).toBeGreaterThan(0.5);

— the floor a spliced velocity discontinuity must clear on noiseless analytic
series built inside that test, with no dependence on any recorded scenario.
That assertion entered in commit `a8531503`; finding F9, which first quoted
measured jumpRatios, entered one commit later in `65199835`. So the number
provably predates the values it is applied to. `git show
a8531503:tests/botbench/kinematicProfile.test.js` is the check.

Two guards run alongside it. A LABEL-BLIND secondary threshold, a Tukey upper
fence over all measured values, which never sees the anomalous/mundane labels
and so cannot be tuned to them (it lands at 5.21, above every value in the set,
and alarms on nothing). And a full sweep over a fixed threshold grid, printed in
its entirety so the free parameter's leverage is visible rather than hidden
behind one favorable choice. No row in that sweep reaches p < 0.05; the lowest
is p = 0.298 at threshold 0.30, where 6/6 anomalies alarm alongside 15/20
mundane scenarios — not a separation in any useful direction.

## The numbers

All 26 records regenerated; none skipped.

At threshold 0.5, exact (Clopper-Pearson) 95% intervals:

| | rate | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| declared anomalous, alarmed | 5/6 (83%) | 36-100% |
| mundane, alarmed | 14/20 (70%) | 46-88% |

Fisher exact, two-sided: **p = 1.0**.

The burst probe stays quiet — **0.173**, the 5th percentile of the mundane
distribution (min 0.160, median 2.105, max 2.770), second-lowest of all 20
mundane scenarios, and it does not alarm at either threshold. That part of F9
survives. It is just not worth much when the alarm also fires on 14 other
mundane scenarios.

Distributions: declared anomalous n=6, 0.339 to 2.499, median 1.893. Mundane
n=20, 0.160 to 2.770, median 2.105. The anomalous range sits entirely inside
the mundane range, and the mundane MEDIAN is the higher of the two.

## Why it fails: the rate series is mostly pointing noise

`kinematicProfile` computes the rate as the angle between CONSECUTIVE
sightlines times fps. Raise fps and the true inter-frame motion shrinks
proportionally while the pointing error does not, so above some sample rate the
angle between consecutive samples is largely noise. `maxJump / medRate` then
measures the extreme-to-median ratio of a Rayleigh noise draw over a few
hundred frames — a number near 2 to 3 that carries no information about the
trajectory.

The report quantifies this per scenario as `motion/noise`: the observed median
rate divided by the rate a perfectly still target would show from realized
pointing noise alone at that sample rate (2-D RMS error x sqrt(2 ln 2) x fps).
1.0 means pure noise.

| fps | scenarios | jumpRatio median | motion/noise median |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | 0.244 | 18.0 |
| 2 | 3 | 0.339 | 10.7 |
| 5 | 1 | 1.230 | 3.1 |
| 10 | 17 | 2.353 | 1.5 |

Spearman(jumpRatio, fps) = **0.83**. Spearman(jumpRatio, declared anomalous) =
**-0.01**.

The decisive comparison, all three at 10 Hz and 0.03 deg declared noise:

- `static-point` (mundane, a target that never moves): **2.603**
- `straight-ca` (mundane, straight constant acceleration): **2.770**
- `hypersonic-glide` (declared anomalous): **2.499**

A stationary target and a straight line both score above the hypersonic glide.
Any reading of this number as an anomaly channel has to survive that.

Why the pilot looked clean: of F9's five quoted cases, four are 1-2 Hz
real-arm scenarios and the fifth — the hypersonic glide — was the only 10 Hz
case among them. A confound that sorts by sample rate is invisible in a set with
one member on the far side of it.

The five values reproduce exactly, so this is a wider measurement of the same
quantity and not a different one: gofast-anom 0.587 (F9: 0.6), hover-anom 0.339
(0.3), hypersonic-glide 2.499 (2.5), gofast 0.160 (0.2), burst 0.173 (0.2).

## What survives, and what would test it

The one structure left is a subgroup, and it is reported as a hypothesis with
its ceiling attached rather than as a result. Among the 8 scenarios sampled at
2 Hz or below — the ones whose rate series still carries motion — the two
declared anomalies rank 1st and 3rd of 8, and at threshold 0.5 the split is 1/2
anomalous against 0/6 mundane, Fisher p = 0.25. The subgroup boundary was
chosen AFTER seeing the confound, so it carries none of the pre-registration
protection the primary threshold has. Its ceiling: with 2 anomalies against 6
mundane scenarios, even a perfect split would only reach p = 0.036.

What would turn it into evidence is a low-rate replication arm — several more
spliced-impulse pairs recorded at 1-2 Hz, pre-registered at this same
threshold. That is a scenario-generation job, not an analysis one.

Two design consequences fall out for the composite-null detector work
(programme steps 17, 23), if the statistic is kept as a candidate component:

- it must be normalized against the clip's pointing-noise floor, not only
  against its own median rate, or it will keep reading sample rate;
- it should be reported only where `motion/noise` is comfortably above 1, and
  declared uninformative otherwise.

## Honesty notes

- Single seed per scenario, designed scenarios. Every rate here is about these
  realizations, not a population; the intervals price counting uncertainty only,
  not scenario-selection uncertainty.
- The statistic self-normalizes by the clip's median rate, which is pinned by
  test: the same physical discontinuity scores LOWER the earlier in the clip it
  happens.
- `declared anomalous` mixes spliced velocity impulses in the real arm
  (gofast-anom, hover-anom) with anomalous PERFORMANCE shapes in the maneuver
  arm (instant turn, zigzag, high-g turn, hypersonic glide). Those are not the
  same physical signature, and there is no reason one statistic should catch
  both. Pooling them is what the declared label does; the per-scenario table is
  where the distinction is readable.
- One premise in the work request was wrong and is recorded as such: the
  statistic was NOT previously computed over all 26 scenarios. Only
  `dossiers.bench.test.js` computed it, over the 8 cases in its `WANT` list,
  which is consistent with F9's own "measured over the eight pilot cases".
  Nothing depended on the wrong premise; this note is the first run over the
  full set.
